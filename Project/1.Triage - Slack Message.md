# Triage - Slack Message (FinBridge Floor 6, 2026-08-14 09:14)

The Slack message bundles three distinct problems into one report, plus a change event that is not itself a problem but is the suspected common variable. Treating them as one issue would misdirect the first checks. Separated below, in order of urgency.

## Problem 1 (Most urgent): Copilot showed a paralegal a client matter she has never had access to
Why most urgent:
- This is not a productivity issue, it is a potential confidentiality/conflicts-of-interest (ethical wall) breach at a law firm. The exposure may already have happened and could be ongoing for other users right now. Every minute it's left unchecked, the possible blast radius (who else can see the matter) stays open.

What to check first:
- The paralegal's effective permissions (direct grants + group inheritance) on that specific matter's library/site - not Copilot itself. Copilot only surfaces content the account already has access to, so the fault is in the permission, not the AI tool.

Why this check first:
- It immediately tells you whether this is a real, live over-permission (requiring instant access removal and a Legal/Compliance escalation) versus something else (e.g. mislabeled matter). Checking Copilot settings or logs first would waste time on the wrong layer and delay containment of a live confidentiality risk.

## Problem 2 (Urgent): A dozen+ people can't log in, or login is very slow
Why urgent:
- Blocks people from working right now, on a floor of 45, first thing Monday morning. Large-enough scale that it likely has one shared mechanism rather than 12 unrelated individual faults.

What to check first:
- Entra ID sign-in logs for a handful of the affected users - specifically the failure/error reason code at the exact time they tried to log in (e.g. Conditional Access block, device-compliance block, credential/MFA failure, timeout).

Why this check first:
- The error code immediately narrows this to one of a few known mechanisms (device compliance state post-migration, Conditional Access policy, or an unrelated auth fault) without guessing. Jumping straight to "reboot devices" or "check the new app" risks fixing the wrong thing for 45 people.

## Problem 3 (Least urgent): Desktop shortcuts have vanished for some users
Why least urgent:
- Cosmetic/friction only - people can still work, and it has a simple user-facing workaround (recreate shortcuts) in the meantime. No confidentiality or availability risk.

What to check first:
- The new document management app's Intune deployment/install script - specifically whether it writes to the public desktop, Start layout, or default profile folders during install.

Why this check first:
- Missing shortcuts after a Friday app rollout is a classic installer side-effect (overwritten Start layout/profile default). Checking the install script directly confirms or rules this out fastest, rather than inspecting individual user profiles one by one.

## The non-problem that ties them together: Friday's document management app rollout
This is the one known change common to the timing and population of all three problems above, which is why it's listed - but it is a candidate shared cause to test against each problem individually, not a fourth problem in its own right. Do not assume it explains all three until each check above confirms or rules it out for that specific symptom.
